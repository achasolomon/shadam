Stigma, poor mental health literacy, poverty, cultural misconceptions, limited access to affordable services, inadequate mental health infrastructure, and the shortage of trained professionals continue to prevent many individuals from obtaining timely assessment, treatment, and ongoing support. SHEDAM Mental Health Initiative addresses these challenges through a comprehensive and multidisciplinary approach that promotes mental health awareness, early identification, professional referral, psycho-spiritual support, research, policy advocacy, community engagement, and strategic partnerships. The Initiative works collaboratively with government agencies, healthcare institutions, educational institutions, faith-based organizations, professional bodies, development partners, civil society organizations, and local communities to strengthen mental health systems and expand access to ethical, evidence-based, and compassionate care.

The Initiative also advocates for the rescue, treatment, rehabilitation, family reunification, and community reintegration of persons living with untreated mental illness who have been abandoned or are roaming the streets, working closely with relevant government agencies, healthcare providers, and community stakeholders.

Guided by the motto, "Creating Awareness. Breaking the Stigma. Connecting People to Professional Help," SHEDAM envisions a society where mental health is understood, openly discussed, and valued as an essential component of overall health and human wellbeing. Through sustainable programmes, sound governance, professional excellence, and strategic collaboration, the Initiative seeks to improve mental health outcomes, strengthen families, empower communities, and contribute to national and global efforts to advance mental wellbeing and sustainable development.




INTRODUCTION

Mental health is a fundamental component of overall health and human wellbeing. It influences how individuals think, feel, make decisions, build relationships, cope with stress, contribute to their communities, and realize their full potential. Healthy societies, productive workplaces, stable families, and sustainable national development all depend, in part, on the mental wellbeing of their people.

Despite increasing global recognition of its importance, mental health remains one of the most neglected areas of healthcare in many low- and middle-income countries. According to the World Health Organization (WHO), mental, neurological, and substance use disorders account for a significant proportion of the global burden of disease. In Nigeria, millions of people experience conditions such as depression, anxiety disorders, trauma-related disorders, substance use disorders, and severe mental illnesses, yet only a small percentage receive appropriate professional care.

The treatment gap is driven by several interconnected factors, including inadequate mental health literacy, persistent stigma and discrimination, poverty, limited access to affordable services, shortages of trained mental health professionals, weak healthcare infrastructure, and widespread misconceptions about the causes and treatment of mental illness. In many communities, mental health conditions are attributed solely to spiritual or supernatural causes, causing many individuals to delay or avoid seeking professional assessment and evidence-based treatment.

The consequences extend beyond the individual. Untreated mental health conditions place enormous emotional, social, and economic burdens on families, reduce educational attainment and workplace productivity, increase vulnerability to homelessness, substance misuse, violence, and suicide, and weaken community resilience. Many individuals who could recover and lead productive lives remain untreated because appropriate services are inaccessible, unaffordable, or poorly understood.

Addressing these challenges requires a coordinated, multidisciplinary, and community-based response involving healthcare professionals, educators, policymakers, researchers, faith leaders, development partners, civil society organizations, families, and communities. Mental health should be recognized not only as a healthcare issue but also as a public health, social development, and human rights priority that demands sustained investment, collaboration, and collective responsibility.

It is within this context that SHEDAM Mental Health Initiative was established to contribute to improving mental health awareness, encouraging prevention and early intervention, facilitating access to professional mental healthcare, strengthening collaboration among stakeholders, supporting vulnerable populations, and advocating for policies that promote the dignity, wellbeing, and inclusion of every individual.




WHO WE ARE

SHEDAM Mental Health Initiative (SMHI) is a non-profit, non-governmental, multidisciplinary organization dedicated to promoting mental wellbeing through mental health awareness, education, prevention, early intervention, advocacy, research, professional referral, and improved access to quality mental healthcare.

The Initiative was founded by Rev. Fr. Istifanus Sheyin, a Clinical Psychologist, and Rev. Fr. Dr. Christopher Damina, a professional in Guidance and Counselling. As Catholic priests with extensive pastoral experience and professional training in mental health, the founders have witnessed firsthand the growing psychological, emotional, social, and spiritual challenges affecting individuals, families, schools, workplaces, and communities. Their experience has shown that while faith and spiritual guidance remain important sources of hope, healing, and resilience, many individuals also require timely, evidence-based psychological and psychiatric care.

SHEDAM Mental Health Initiative was therefore established to complement—not replace—pastoral care by promoting professional mental healthcare while recognizing the positive contribution of healthy spirituality to holistic human wellbeing. The Initiative affirms that faith and science are not competing approaches but complementary resources that, when responsibly integrated within appropriate professional and ethical boundaries, contribute significantly to the wellbeing and recovery of individuals experiencing mental health challenges.

SHEDAM brings together clinical psychologists, psychiatrists, counsellors, guidance and counselling professionals, psychiatric nurses, social workers, psycho-spiritual care practitioners, educators, researchers, public health professionals, and other mental health practitioners who share a common commitment to advancing mental wellbeing through multidisciplinary collaboration.

Recognizing that mental wellbeing is influenced by biological, psychological, social, cultural, and spiritual factors, the Initiative adopts a holistic approach that integrates evidence-based mental healthcare with appropriate psycho-spiritual support while respecting professional ethics, scientific standards, and the beliefs and values of every individual.

Beyond creating awareness, SHEDAM serves as a trusted platform for connecting individuals and families with qualified mental health professionals, supporting vulnerable and underserved populations, strengthening mental health systems, promoting research and innovation, building professional capacity, and advocating for policies that improve access to quality mental healthcare throughout Nigeria and beyond. Therefore, guided by the conviction that mental health is a fundamental human right and an essential component of overall health and human wellbeing, SHEDAM is committed to building healthier individuals, stronger families, and more resilient communities through compassion, professionalism, collaboration, innovation, and sustainable action.



THE NEED FOR SHEDAM MENTAL HEALTH INITIATIVE

The establishment of SHEDAM Mental Health Initiative is a response to the growing mental health needs of individuals, families, and communities. Across Nigeria and many developing societies, rapid social change, poverty, unemployment, insecurity, displacement, family instability, substance abuse, academic pressure, workplace stress, trauma, violence, and social isolation continue to increase the prevalence of mental health conditions and the demand for accessible mental healthcare.

Although public awareness of mental health is gradually improving, many people still experience significant barriers to obtaining appropriate care. Persistent stigma and discrimination, misinformation, cultural misconceptions, financial hardship, limited mental health services, and the shortage of qualified mental health professionals continue to prevent countless individuals from receiving timely assessment, treatment, and ongoing support. As a result, many people suffer in silence, while others remain untreated until their conditions become severe.
Particularly affected are economically disadvantaged individuals who cannot afford psychological or psychiatric care, as well as persons living with untreated mental illness who have been abandoned, displaced, or left to roam the streets without access to treatment, rehabilitation, or family support. These situations represent not only public health concerns but also humanitarian and human dignity challenges that require coordinated and compassionate responses.

Addressing these realities demands more than clinical services alone. It requires sustained public education, stigma reduction, early identification, professional referral systems, community engagement, policy advocacy, research, workforce development, and strong collaboration among healthcare professionals, government institutions, educational institutions, faith communities, development partners, civil society organizations, families, and local communities.

SHEDAM Mental Health Initiative was established to respond to these needs through a holistic, multidisciplinary, and evidence-based approach to mental health promotion and care. The Initiative is committed to improving mental health literacy, facilitating access to qualified professionals, supporting vulnerable populations, strengthening mental health systems, promoting research and professional development, and advocating for policies that ensure equitable access to quality mental healthcare. SHEDAM believes that no individual should be denied the opportunity to live a healthy and productive life because of ignorance, stigma, discrimination, or inability to afford treatment. Through strategic partnerships and sustainable interventions, the Initiative seeks to restore hope, protect human dignity, strengthen families, and contribute to healthier, more inclusive, and more resilient communities.

THEORY OF CHANGE

SHEDAM Mental Health Initiative believes that lasting improvements in mental health require coordinated action across the entire continuum of care—from awareness and prevention to treatment, recovery, and community reintegration.
We believe that when individuals and communities receive accurate mental health education, stigma is reduced, mental health conditions are recognized earlier, more people seek professional help, access to treatment improves, recovery rates increase, and individuals are better able to reintegrate into family and community life. Accordingly, SHEDAM's programmes are guided by the following pathway:
Mental Health Awareness → Early Identification → Professional Referral → Assessment and Treatment → Recovery and Rehabilitation → Social Reintegration → Improved Community Wellbeing
This framework informs the planning, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of all SHEDAM programmes and provides the foundation for measuring organizational impact and promoting sustainable mental health outcomes.

AREAS OF INTERVENTION

SHEDAM Mental Health Initiative adopts a holistic, multidisciplinary, and evidence-based approach to promoting mental wellbeing and improving access to quality mental healthcare. Recognizing that mental health is influenced by psychological, biological, social, cultural, economic, and spiritual factors, the Initiative implements programmes across the following strategic areas of intervention.

1. Mental Health Awareness and Public Education

Mental health awareness is the foundation of SHEDAM's work. The Initiative promotes mental health literacy, encourages early help-seeking behaviour, and challenges stigma, discrimination, myths, and misconceptions through sustained public education and advocacy.
Programmes include conferences, seminars, workshops, public lectures, webinars, community outreaches, media campaigns, radio and television programmes, digital media engagement, publications, advocacy campaigns, and the development of culturally appropriate educational resources. Particular attention is given to children, adolescents, parents, teachers, healthcare workers, workplaces, faith communities, security agencies, correctional institutions, and underserved rural communities.
2. Professional Referral and Clinical Support
SHEDAM serves as a trusted referral platform connecting individuals and families with qualified psychologists, psychiatrists, counsellors, psychiatric nurses, social workers, rehabilitation centres, and accredited healthcare institutions.
The Initiative facilitates timely assessment, counselling, psychotherapy, psychiatric care, rehabilitation, crisis intervention, and follow-up services through established referral pathways. All referrals are guided by professional ethics, confidentiality, informed consent, respect for human dignity, and evidence-based practice.

3. Psycho-Spiritual Care

SHEDAM recognizes that spirituality can be an important source of hope, resilience, and healing for many individuals. Accordingly, the Initiative promotes responsible collaboration between mental health professionals and faith leaders to support holistic wellbeing while maintaining professional standards and ethical practice. This entails that Shedam believes that therapeutic intervention may require a joint effort of both professionals and faith leaders.
Psycho-spiritual support complements—but never replaces—professional mental healthcare and is always provided in a manner that respects the beliefs, values, autonomy, and dignity of every individual.

4. Mental Healthcare Support for the Indigent

SHEDAM believes that financial hardship should never prevent anyone from accessing quality mental healthcare.
Working with government agencies, healthcare institutions, charitable organizations, corporate partners, philanthropic foundations, and individual donors, the Initiative supports economically disadvantaged individuals through free or subsidized psychological assessments, counselling, psychotherapy, psychiatric consultations, medication support, rehabilitation services, transportation assistance, and follow-up care, subject to available resources.
5. Rescue, Rehabilitation and Community Reintegration
SHEDAM is committed to restoring dignity and hope to persons living with untreated mental illness who have been abandoned, displaced, or are living on the streets.
In collaboration with government agencies, psychiatric hospitals, rehabilitation centres, social welfare departments, community organizations, security agencies, and families, the Initiative supports rescue, assessment, treatment, rehabilitation, family tracing, reunification, vocational support, and community reintegration. This intervention reflects SHEDAM's commitment to protecting vulnerable persons, promoting recovery, and affirming the inherent dignity and value of every human life.

6. School and University Mental Health Programmes

SHEDAM recognizes that schools, colleges, and universities play a critical role in promoting mental wellbeing and supporting early identification of mental health challenges among children, adolescents, and young adults.
The Initiative shall collaborate with educational institutions and relevant authorities to implement comprehensive mental health programmes that promote safe, inclusive, and supportive learning environments. These programmes may include:
Mental health awareness campaigns.
Student counselling and referral services.
Teacher and staff capacity-building.
Parent education programmes.
Peer support initiatives.
Mental Health Clubs.
Resilience, emotional wellbeing, and life skills training.
Anti-bullying and anti-cyberbullying programmes.
Suicide prevention awareness.
Early identification and referral systems.
Development of institutional mental health policies and support systems.
Through these interventions, SHEDAM seeks to strengthen the mental wellbeing of learners while supporting educational institutions in creating healthier and more supportive learning communities.

7. Psychology Education and Mental Health Workforce Development

A strong mental health system depends on a well-trained and adequately supported workforce. SHEDAM is committed to strengthening professional education and expanding opportunities for workforce development in psychology and other mental health disciplines.
The Initiative shall collaborate with universities, educational authorities, accreditation bodies, professional associations, healthcare institutions, and development partners to support the establishment and strengthening of psychology programmes and other mental health-related disciplines where needed.
SHEDAM shall also promote scholarships, mentorship, internships, continuing professional development, leadership training, research opportunities, and professional networking to help build a competent, ethical, and sustainable mental health workforce capable of meeting present and future needs.

8. Research, Policy Development, and Advocacy

Evidence-based research and effective public policy are essential for improving mental healthcare and strengthening mental health systems.
SHEDAM shall conduct, support, and disseminate research on emerging mental health issues to inform programme design, public education, policy formulation, and professional practice. Research findings shall guide advocacy initiatives and contribute to national and international discussions on mental health.
The Initiative shall advocate for:
	Stronger mental health legislation.
	Increased public investment in mental healthcare.
	Integration of mental health services into primary healthcare.
	Protection of the rights and dignity of persons living with mental health conditions.
	Expansion of community-based mental health services.
	Improved working conditions for mental health professionals.
	Increased investment in mental health education, research, and workforce development.
Through strategic advocacy and stakeholder engagement, SHEDAM seeks to influence policies and systems that improve equitable access to quality mental healthcare.

9. Mental Health Emergency Response and Crisis Intervention

Mental health emergencies require timely, coordinated, and compassionate responses. SHEDAM shall develop programmes that provide psychological first aid, crisis intervention, suicide prevention awareness, trauma counselling, bereavement support, emergency referrals, and post-disaster psychosocial services.
The Initiative shall collaborate with hospitals, emergency response agencies, humanitarian organizations, educational institutions, faith communities, and government agencies to provide mental health support during emergencies, disasters, conflicts, disease outbreaks, accidents, and other humanitarian crises.
Where appropriate, SHEDAM may establish and train crisis response teams to provide immediate psychological support and facilitate rapid referral to specialized mental health professionals.
Through these interventions, the Initiative seeks to save lives, reduce psychological suffering, strengthen resilience, and promote recovery during times of crisis.

General Provisions
The Permanent Standing Sub-Committees shall constitute the operational backbone of SHEDAM and shall be responsible for supporting the planning, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of all seminars, workshops, conferences, advocacy programmes, outreach activities, research projects, and every other programme organized by the Initiative.
All committees shall work collaboratively with one another, the Executive Management Team, the Founders, and the Board of Directors to ensure the successful realization of SHEDAM's vision and mission.
Each committee shall submit periodic reports of its activities and recommendations to the Executive Management Team and the Founders.
Members shall uphold the highest standards of professionalism, accountability, integrity, confidentiality, teamwork, transparency, and ethical conduct.
The Founders and the Board of Directors reserve the right to review the composition, mandate, and Terms of Reference of any Standing Sub-Committee whenever necessary to enhance the effectiveness, sustainability, and impact of the SHEDAM Mental Health Initiative.

CODE OF ETHICS AND PROFESSIONAL CONDUCT

All Trustees, officers, employees, consultants, volunteers, interns, and representatives of SHEDAM Mental Health Initiative shall conduct themselves with integrity, professionalism, impartiality, and respect for human dignity.

Every person serving the Initiative shall:
	Respect the dignity, rights, privacy, and autonomy of every individual.
	Maintain confidentiality in accordance with applicable laws and professional ethics.
	Avoid discrimination, harassment, exploitation, abuse, or intimidation.
	Promote evidence-based practice.
	Act honestly and responsibly in the management of organizational resources.
	Avoid conflicts of interest.
	Protect confidential organizational information.
	Comply with all applicable laws and organizational policies.
	Promote teamwork, mutual respect, and professionalism.

Professional services provided under the auspices of the Initiative shall be delivered only by qualified persons acting within the limits of their competence and in accordance with applicable professional standards.

SAFEGUARDING POLICY
SHEDAM Mental Health Initiative is committed to providing a safe, respectful, and inclusive environment for all persons who engage with its programmes and activities.

The Initiative shall adopt and implement safeguarding policies designed to protect:
Children.
Vulnerable adults.
Persons living with disabilities.
Persons living with mental health conditions.
Staff.
Volunteers.
Beneficiaries.
Partners.

The Initiative shall maintain zero tolerance for all forms of abuse, neglect, exploitation, sexual harassment, discrimination, violence, and harmful practices. Appropriate safeguarding procedures, reporting mechanisms, investigations, and disciplinary measures shall be established and implemented.


CONFIDENTIALITY AND DATA PROTECTION

The Initiative recognizes the sensitive nature of personal and health-related information.
All personal information obtained in the course of programme implementation, counselling, research, referrals, treatment, monitoring, or administration shall be collected, processed, stored, used, and disclosed only in accordance with applicable laws, professional ethics, and organizational policies.

Confidential information shall not be disclosed except:
	with the informed consent of the individual concerned;
	where disclosure is required by law;
	where disclosure is necessary to prevent serious and imminent harm;
	or where otherwise permitted by applicable professional standards.

The Initiative shall implement appropriate administrative, technical, and physical safeguards to protect confidential information from unauthorized access, disclosure, alteration, or destruction.

CONFLICT OF INTEREST
Every Board member, Trustee, officer, employee, volunteer, consultant, and committee member shall avoid situations in which personal interests conflict, or may reasonably appear to conflict, with the interests of SHEDAM Mental Health Initiative.
Any actual, potential, or perceived conflict of interest shall be disclosed promptly.
A person with a declared conflict shall not participate in deliberations or decision-making relating to the matter unless expressly permitted by the Board after full disclosure.
The Board shall adopt and periodically review a Conflict of Interest Policy to guide implementation of this provision.

FINANCIAL ACCOUNTABILITY

The financial affairs of SHEDAM Mental Health Initiative shall be managed with the highest standards of transparency, accountability, prudence, and integrity.
Accordingly:
Proper books of accounts shall be maintained.
Annual budgets shall be approved by the Board.
Expenditure shall be authorized in accordance with approved financial policies.
Annual financial statements shall be prepared.
Independent external audits shall be conducted annually or as otherwise required by law.
Financial reports shall be presented to the Board and relevant regulatory authorities.
Internal financial controls shall be maintained to safeguard organizational assets and prevent fraud.
No organizational funds shall be used for purposes inconsistent with the objectives of the Initiative.

RISK MANAGEMENT
The Initiative shall establish systems for identifying, assessing, monitoring, and managing strategic, operational, financial, legal, reputational, technological, and safeguarding risks.
The Board shall periodically review the organizational risk register and ensure that appropriate mitigation measures are implemented.

AMENDMENT OF THIS CONSTITUTION
This Constitution may be amended whenever it becomes necessary to improve the governance, effectiveness, or sustainability of SHEDAM Mental Health Initiative.
Any proposal for amendment shall:
be submitted in writing to the Board of Trustees;
clearly state the provisions to be amended and the reasons for the proposed amendment; and be circulated to eligible members or Trustees before the meeting at which it is to be considered. An amendment shall take effect only upon approval by not less than two-thirds (2/3) of the members entitled to vote at a duly constituted meeting.

DISSOLUTION
SHEDAM Mental Health Initiative may be dissolved only by a resolution supported by not less than two-thirds (2/3) of the members entitled to vote at a duly constituted meeting convened specifically for that purpose.
Upon dissolution, and after the settlement of all lawful debts and liabilities, any remaining assets shall not be distributed to any Trustee, member, founder, officer, or employee.
Such assets shall be transferred to one or more non-profit organizations in Nigeria with similar charitable objectives, in accordance with applicable laws and the resolution approving the dissolution.

INTERPRETATION
Any question concerning the interpretation of the Constitution shall, in the first instance, be determined by the Board of Trustees.
Where necessary, the Board may obtain independent legal or professional advice to ensure that the interpretation is consistent with applicable laws and the objectives of the Initiative.

COMMENCEMENT
This Constitution shall come into force on the date of its adoption by the founding members of SHEDAM Mental Health Initiative and shall remain binding upon all Trustees, officers, members, employees, volunteers, and representatives of the Initiative until amended in accordance with its provisions.
All persons associated with SHEDAM Mental Health Initiative shall uphold and faithfully observe the provisions of this Constitution in the discharge of their respective responsibilities.
